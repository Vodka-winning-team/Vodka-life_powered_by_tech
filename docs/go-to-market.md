# Verifye: how we would launch it, market it and fund it

Owner: D (Paveesh). **This is a plan, not a record.** We have no partnership, no customer and no price agreed with anyone. We say that on stage.

## 1. In one line

> We launch with job seekers first, through the places they already are, keep the app free for people, and ask the institutions that fraud costs money to fund it.

## 2. Who it is for, and what each needs to hear

| Audience | Who | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Main: job seekers, 18–35** | People like Kevin, looking for work abroad or online | "Never pay to get a job. Check first." |
| **Second: their parents** | The ones asked to lend the money | "Before you lend it, check it." |
| **Third: institutions** | Ministry of Labour, CERT-MU, MAUCORS, banks | "It makes your own lists and warnings usable at the moment someone is about to pay." |

- **The line:** *Verifye avan ou peye.* Check before you pay.
- **The promise:** in 10 seconds, see what the law and the official lists say about an offer.
- **The tone:** a family member giving advice, in Kreol. Not a bank, not the police.

## 3. Rollout in three phases

| Phase | When | What happens | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Pilot** | Months 1–2 | 50–100 job seekers and parents, through one employment information office and 2–3 Facebook job groups. Real user tests (we ran none this weekend), Kreol fixes. | Do people check before paying? |
| **2. Launch** | Months 3–6 | Free on Google Play. Short Kreol videos, posters, one radio slot. Present it to the Ministry's recruitment licence unit, CERT-MU and MAUCORS. | A few thousand installs; one official partner |
| **3. Handover** | Months 6–12 | An institution hosts the server and keeps the lists fresh. iPhone through the App Store. | It runs without the four of us |

## 4. Where we reach people

- **Where the offers appear:** Facebook job groups and WhatsApp. The app's own "share a summary" spreads it family to family.
- **Short videos** (20 seconds, Kreol): a message arrives → Verifye says Stop → the voice reads it out.
- **Where job seekers go:** employment information offices, job fairs, youth centres, bank branches. A poster with a QR code: "Job offer abroad? *Verifye avan ou peye.*"
- **A one-page Kreol leaflet:** "7 costs the employer pays, not you."
- **Radio:** the cases we cite came from Radio One; a short mention reaches parents.
- **Institutions:** a link to Verifye from their own warnings.
- **No paid ads at the start.** The audience is already in free groups.

## 5. How it is funded: free for people, paid for by institutions

The app can't charge users, show ads or sell data: Kevin is being asked for Rs 50,000 he doesn't have, and ads in a fraud-checking app would sometimes be the fraud.

| Who would pay | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Banks and mobile-money providers** | Fraud costs them refunds, staff time and reputation | Sponsorship, then a licence for our checks inside their own apps |
| **A public body** (Ministry of Labour, CERT-MU, MAUCORS) | Warning the public is their job; the app makes their lists usable | A service contract or a grant |
| **Telecom operators** | Fraud travels over their SMS and data | Sponsorship and distribution |
| **Grants and competitions** | Digital-safety and financial-inclusion funds | Seed money for the pilot year |

**The order:** year 1, a grant- or sponsor-funded pilot → year 2, licensing the checks to banks → long term, a public body hosts the free app.

## 6. Banks and mobile-money providers, in detail

**Why they would pay.** The moment before the payment happens outside the bank's app, in WhatsApp or SMS. The bank can't see it; Verifye can.

| What fraud costs a bank | How Verifye helps |
|---|---|
| Refunds and disputes | Fewer customers send the money in the first place |
| Staff time (fraud lines, branches, complaints) | Fewer cases |
| Reputation: fake messages use the bank's name | Verifye says "this is not the bank's official site" and gives the real number |
| Regulatory pressure | They can show the Bank of Mauritius they protect customers |

**Who.** The 18 banks on the Bank of Mauritius list in our data, and the 3 payment apps in our data (MCB Juice, my.t money, blink by Emtel): job fees are often asked for "by Juice". One door for all banks at once: the Mauritius Bankers Association.

**What we would offer them.**
1. **Sponsorship of the free app** (simplest): "Protected with [bank]" in the app and on posters; the bank promotes it. A yearly fee. Right for the pilot year.
2. **Our checks inside their own app** (the real product): a "Check a message" button in the banking app, powered by our engine. It is plain rules plus lists, no AI model, so it is small, explainable and easy for a security team to review. A yearly licence, plus keeping the lists current.
3. **"Before you pay" at the payment screen:** when a customer is about to send money to a new account, the app asks "Were you asked for this by message? Check it first." Our pay check is a first version. The most valuable placement: the last point where the payment can still be stopped.
4. **Official-number and official-link data:** a maintained list of every bank's real domains and numbers, which we already built and checked.
5. **A report feed** (later, with care): confirmed reports of messages misusing a bank's name, as fingerprints only, nothing about the person who reported.

**Pricing.** Not set. We would price it in the pilot, against what one prevented fraud case saves a bank.

**How the sale would go.** Pilot results first → one champion bank for a 3-month trial → their security review of our engine → then the others.

**Risks and objections.**
- *"We already warn our customers."* Inside your app and by SMS, yes. The fake message arrives in WhatsApp, where you aren't.
- **Neutrality:** a sponsor's bank is checked exactly like every other bank.
- **Trust:** banks are cautious with a student project; a public body's backing makes it easier.
- **Liability:** the app never says "safe", only "no warning signs found – still check".
- **Slow decisions:** banks take months, which is why year 1 is grants and a pilot.

## 7. What we must never do

- Charge users, or sell "premium" checks: no paid tier of safety.
- Show ads, or sell data.
- Let anyone pay to look trustworthy: a "listed" result depends only on the official register.

## 8. How we would measure it

- How many users tap "Call the official number" (or stop) after a Stop result: our main measure.
- Installs and checks per week; shares; reports confirmed by two phones.
- Which channel each install came from (a different QR code per poster location).
- We don't claim "money saved": we can't prove it.

## 9. What to say on stage

**"How will you roll it out?" (20 s)**
> "We go where the fake offers are: Facebook job groups and WhatsApp. We go where job seekers are: employment offices and job fairs, with a poster and a QR code. And we ask the institutions whose data we use to point people to it. We start with a pilot of 100 real users, because we haven't tested with outside users yet."

**"How do you make money?" (15 s)**
> "Verifye stays free for users. Fraud costs banks and the state real money, so they would fund it: first a sponsored pilot, then licensing our checks into banking apps. We haven't signed anyone; that's the plan after the pilot."

**"Why would a bank pay?" (15 s)**
> "Fake messages use banks' names, and the payment often goes out by Juice or my.t money. Banks pay for that in refunds and complaints. We'd put our checks inside their own apps, right before the payment: one bank as a pilot, then a yearly licence."
